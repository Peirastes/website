Model artifact vs physiology
In the model
Tank A drains and refills through one pipe, so recovery is close to a single exponential with τ_O = A_P(1−φ)/M_O, plus the optional lag τ_x.
In the literature
PCr recovery is often described as biphasic; reported half-times differ between protocols (see Harris 1976, Bogdanis 1995).
In the model
At light activity tank A settles below 100%, because the head difference is what drives oxidative flow.
In the literature
Resting PCr is near full; the tank level is a model state, not a measured concentration.
In the model
Tank L refills only through a slow return pipe; its level is not blood lactate.
In the literature
Blood lactate reflects production, exchange and clearance, and lactate is also oxidised as a fuel (Brooks 2018).
In the model
No glycogen, heat, dehydration or neuromuscular fatigue. The optional extension adds two assumed terms.
In the literature
Long-match fatigue involves several mechanisms (Hornery 2007, Girard 2008).
In the model
A rally is a square pulse at constant intensity; power is mechanical-equivalent and expressed as % MAP.
In the literature
On-court demand is a sequence of accelerations, strokes and changes of direction; mechanical power is not measured directly.
Overview

Three connected tanks stand for the phosphagen (A), glycolytic (L) and oxidative (O) systems, after Morton (1986). The match structure becomes a demand signal; the tanks deliver it until the tap limit is reached. Every parameter is illustrative.

Tank A balance
AP·dh/dt = P(t) − ṖO − ṖG + ṖR
Tank L balance
AG·dl/dt = ṖG − ṖR
Oxidative flow
ṖO = MO·min(h, 1−φ)/(1−φ)
Glycolytic pipes
ṖG = gG·max(0, h−θ−l)
ṖR = gR·max(0, θ+l−h)
Tap limit
P ≤ MP·(1 − h)
Low-load recovery
τO = AP(1−φ)/MO,   t½ = τ·ln2

How to use these

Each question links to concept nodes and sources. Some load a setup into the simulator. They are prompts for your own reading and reasoning; the simulator shows what this model does, which is not the same as what a player's body does.

A useful habit: for any pattern you see in the simulator, ask which line of the model produces it, then look for a measurement in the library that could confirm or contradict it.
