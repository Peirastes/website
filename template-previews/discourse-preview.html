Senior design presentation: PID control of an aerodynamically-driven inverted pendulum.

Dynamic Control of an Aeropendulum

Venue: University of Central Oklahoma — ENGR Senior Design
Date: Spring 2020
Audience: Engineering Faculty & Industry Sponsors
Download: ⬇ 📊

Can a student-built aeropendulum achieve stable inverted balance using only PID control and a single DC motor-driven propeller?

The aeropendulum — sometimes called a propendulum — is a deceptively simple apparatus: a rigid arm, free to rotate about a horizontal pivot, with a propeller mounted at one end. The propeller generates a thrust force that, when properly modulated, can hold the arm at any desired angle, including the inherently unstable inverted (upward) position. What makes this system compelling as a senior design project is that it sits at the intersection of dynamics, control theory, embedded systems, and fabrication. The governing equation of motion is a nonlinear second-order ODE, the control law must account for gravitational torque that varies sinusoidally with angle, and the physical build demands tight tolerances to minimize friction and vibration.

Our team designed, 3D-printed, and assembled the pendulum frame, motor mount, and pivot housing from PLA. An MPU-6050 inertial measurement unit mounted at the pivot provides angular position and rate feedback at 100 Hz. The Arduino Mega reads the IMU data, computes the PID control output, and drives the brushless DC motor through an ESC (electronic speed controller). The control loop runs at 50 Hz — fast enough to stabilize the pendulum but slow enough to avoid aliasing the IMU data. Tuning the PID gains proved to be the most time-consuming phase of the project: the nonlinear gravity torque means that gains optimized for small perturbations near the inverted equilibrium produce unacceptable overshoot during large-angle recovery maneuvers. We addressed this with a gain-scheduled PID controller that switches between two gain sets depending on the angular error magnitude.

The final system achieved stable inverted balance with a settling time of approximately 1.2 seconds and steady-state oscillation amplitude under 2 degrees. Disturbance rejection was tested by applying impulse perturbations (a finger flick) and step disturbances (a small mass added to the arm). In both cases the controller recovered to the setpoint within 3 seconds. The project demonstrated that classical PID control, when carefully tuned and augmented with gain scheduling, is sufficient for stabilizing a first-order inverted pendulum — no state-space or optimal control methods are required. This makes the aeropendulum an excellent teaching tool: it is complex enough to be interesting, but simple enough that students can understand every layer of the system from physics to firmware.

System Specifications

Motor

  • DC Brushless Outrunner
  • 2200 KV rating
  • 30A ESC speed controller
  • 8x4.5" two-blade propeller

Controller

  • Arduino Mega 2560
  • 50 Hz control loop
  • Gain-scheduled PID
  • Serial data logging at 100 Hz

Sensor

  • MPU-6050 6-axis IMU
  • Complementary filter fusion
  • 100 Hz sample rate
  • ±0.5° angular resolution

Frame

  • 3D-printed PLA structure
  • Ball-bearing pivot joint
  • 450 mm arm length
  • Total mass: 320 g (arm + motor)
