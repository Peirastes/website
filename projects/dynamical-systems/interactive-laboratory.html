📚 What to Look For in Each Regime

↩️ Underdamped (ζ < 1)

Animation: Mass/charge oscillates with decreasing amplitude.
Phase Portrait: Spiral trajectory curving inward toward origin.
Why: Restoring force (spring/capacitor) dominates. Damping is weak.
Application: RLC circuits for filtering, shock absorbers.

➡️ Critically Damped (ζ ≈ 1)

Animation: Mass/charge returns smoothly to rest WITHOUT overshooting.
Phase Portrait: Smooth curve approaching origin, no spiral.
Why: Damping perfectly balances spring stiffness.
Application: Door closers, car suspensions (ideal comfort).

🐢 Overdamped (ζ > 1)

Animation: Mass/charge drifts slowly back without any oscillation.
Phase Portrait: Slow exponential approach along velocity axis.
Why: Damping is so strong it suppresses all oscillation.
Application: Viscous brakes, heavy damped isolation mounts.

🔬 Key Experiment: Parameter Correspondence

  1. Start with "Underdamped" preset. Notice both systems oscillate identically.
  2. Adjust mechanical mass (m) upward. Then adjust electrical inductance (L) by the same ratio.
  3. Observe: Natural frequency ω₀ stays synchronized. Theorem XI.8.1 verified!
  4. Now increase mechanical damping (b). Increase electrical resistance (R) by the same ratio.
  5. Notice: Both systems show identical damping behavior. The analogy is exact, not approximate.
  6. Try changing drive (F or V). Magnitude of response scales identically—because Power = F·v = p·Q!
