# Signal Quality Distribution of Ancient Monuments Under the ECDO Pole Hypothesis

**Observation:** When the 115 monuments in the ECDO Watch dataset are mapped against their signal quality — a measure of azimuth discrimination ability relative to the hypothesized former poles (Np' at 14°S, 31°E and Sp' at 14°N, 149°W) — they exhibit a striking clustering pattern.

**The pattern:** 109 of 115 monuments (94.8%) fall in the yellow-to-blue quality bands (33–100%), meaning they sit far from both hypothesized former poles. Only 6 monuments fall in the low-quality red/amber bands near a pole. This mirrors exactly how modern civilization clusters in temperate latitudes between the current geographic poles.

**The 6 polar outliers:**
- Great Zimbabwe (6.3° from Np'), Adams Calendar (11.5° from Np') — practically on top of the former North Pole
- Lalibela (27.2° from Np'), Aksum (29.2° from Np') — within the former arctic circle equivalent
- Plaine Magnien (26.2° from Np') — Indian Ocean, near Np'
- Punchbowl Crater (11.2° from Sp') — Hawaii, near the former South Pole

**Interpretation:** The distribution is consistent with two eras of construction under two different pole configurations:

1. **Pre-shift monuments (the 94.8%)** — Built when Np'/Sp' were the active poles. Builders lived in temperate zones between those poles (just as we live between today's poles), which naturally placed them in the high signal-quality zone. Alignment toward Np' was feasible because they had strong azimuth discrimination at those latitudes.

2. **Post-shift monuments (the 6 outliers)** — Built after the poles moved to their current positions. Regions like southern Africa and Ethiopia, previously uninhabitable (sitting under or near the former polar ice), became temperate. Structures built there under the *new* pole axis wouldn't be aligned to Np' — and couldn't be, since azimuth discrimination toward the old pole is near zero at those locations.

**The deeper point:** The clustering requires no special explanation beyond "people build where they live, and they live in temperate zones between the poles." But the fact that this mundane pattern holds for the *hypothesized former* pole axis — not just the current one — is itself evidence that Np'/Sp' once governed the Earth's climate zones.

**Robustness:** The pattern was first observed with 97 monuments (94% in temperate bands). After adding 18 monuments from 10+ previously unrepresented regions (Sudan, South Korea, West Africa, Portugal, Balkans, Scandinavia, Tonga, Colombia, India, Greece), the distribution held at 94.8% — none of the new sites fell into the polar zone. The clustering is not an artifact of site selection.
