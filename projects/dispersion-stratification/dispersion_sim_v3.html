The inference shown is a simple quadratic-plus-cubic least-squares fit to the out-group's observed |ψ|²(x, t) history. It is a stylized stand-in for "whatever inference procedure an out-group might use" — not a faithful cognitive model. What's real and visible is the structural mismatch: when β is high and changing, no fixed ω(k) fits the observations, and the out-group's best attempt systematically underestimates the asymmetric suppression of high-k modes.

How to read each mode. Modes share the same two-field physics (Strang-split coupled dispersion); they differ in what auxiliary information is shown and what the user controls. Mode A lets you play the in-group's strategic problem — raise β for rent, but keep the visible gap Δ_vis small enough that belief doesn't catch up to reality. Mode B plays back historical arcs as scripted regime shifts; watch the dispersion operator jump at named events rather than drift smoothly. Mode C visualizes weaponized underdetermination: the out-group fits a polynomial ω(k) to the observed field evolution, and you can watch the gap between true and inferred widen as β becomes adversarial. All three are companions to the paper, not replacements for it — they show what the formalism looks like in motion.
